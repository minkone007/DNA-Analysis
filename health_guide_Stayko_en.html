Deeper Analysis

Stayko's eye colour prediction lands at the same blue-green boundary as his brother's, but arrives there through a slightly different genetic path. HERC2 rs12913832 GG is identical between the two brothers — the single strongest known determinant of human eye colour, and the genotype responsible for roughly 97% of blue-eyed Europeans carrying it. On its own, this predicts blue clearly.

What differs is the secondary pigmentation layer. Minko carries OCA2 CC and TYR AG; Stayko carries OCA2 CT and TYR GG — a different specific combination, though one that lands in a comparable overall place. OCA2 CT is intermediate, leaning blue, essentially the same directional pull as Minko's CC. TYR GG, however, is the ancestral (non-lightening) genotype, in contrast to Minko's heterozygous AG — meaning Stayko's tyrosinase enzyme runs at a slightly higher baseline activity than his brother's at this specific position, a modest push toward more melanin than Minko's version carries here.

The net effect is still a lighter-than-brown iris — HERC2's dominant signal is too strong to be overridden by the secondary loci in either brother's case — but the exact shade this produces may sit a shade further from pure blue and a shade closer to green or hazel-green than Minko's prediction, precisely because the TYR genotype isn't pulling in the same lightening direction. Brown is still effectively ruled out for both brothers; the real question, as with Minko, is exactly where on the blue-to-green spectrum the final result lands, and that depends on iris structure and lighting as much as genotype.

This is the clearest divergence between the two brothers anywhere in the Physical Appearance section, and it comes down to a single X-linked position. Minko carries rs2497938 CC — the non-risk genotype at the dominant AR/EDA2R marker — and his report accordingly reads a reassuringly low hair-loss signal. Stayko is CC at that same rs2497938 position too, but carries TT at a second AR/EDA2R marker, rs2180439, which is the risk allele there.

Because this locus sits on the X chromosome, it is inherited exclusively from the mother — this is not a paternal-line trait, and the difference between the brothers reflects the specific combination of maternal X-chromosome alleles each of them happened to inherit, not any difference in their father's contribution. Male pattern baldness is roughly 80% heritable, making it one of the most strongly genetically determined traits covered in either report, and this particular X-linked signal is one of its best-replicated components.

The overall GWAS picture is modest in absolute terms — 2 elevated loci and a PGS of +4.0, a small polygenic score in the context of this report's full range — but the specific risk allele at rs2180439 is worth being aware of. As with any polygenic prediction, this describes a statistical tendency, not a certainty, and plenty of men carrying this exact genotype combination do not experience significant androgenetic alopecia.

Deeper Analysis

Height remains one of the most polygenic traits in the human genome, and Stayko's overall genome-wide signal — a PGS of +415.5 from 27 elevated loci — is directionally consistent with Minko's own above-average height score, though the two brothers differ at the one well-characterised curated locus available on the chip.

Minko carries HMGA2 rs1042725 as TT, the homozygous taller-associated genotype. Stayko carries CT — heterozygous, meaning he has inherited one copy of the taller-associated T allele and one copy of the shorter-associated C allele. Each T allele contributes approximately 0.4 cm in large studies, so this single locus alone would predict Stayko sitting slightly below Minko's height contribution from this specific gene, all else being equal.

That said, HMGA2 is only one contributor among thousands across the genome, and the aggregate polygenic score — built from the 27 elevated GWAS loci — is where most of the real predictive signal lives. With a broadly similar overall PGS to his brother, Stayko's realised height is likely to land in a similar above-average range, shaped as much by shared childhood nutrition and environment as by this one differing locus.
